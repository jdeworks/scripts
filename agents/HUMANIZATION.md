# Human writing

Apply this policy to every piece of authored text that a reader will see. This includes:

- assistant replies and explanations;
- README and documentation prose;
- UI, SEO and GitHub copy;
- launch posts, descriptions and narration.

Do not apply it to code, commands, identifiers, file paths, URLs, raw logs, machine-readable data or
verbatim source text. Preserve literal technical syntax and quotations exactly.

## Hard rules

1. Use zero em dashes. Rewrite with a period, comma or colon. An en dash is allowed only as a short
   title separator such as "Tool – Tagline".
2. Do not chain thoughts with semicolons. Give each thought its own sentence.
3. Break the "x, y, and z" rhythm. Use two items, use four or restructure the sentence.
4. Do not define something by negation. Replace "not X, but Y" with a direct statement of Y.
5. Do not balance every quality into a hedge such as "simple yet powerful". Choose the quality
   that matters and support it.
6. Remove grandiose filler. Avoid seamless, effortless, powerful, tapestry, testament, elevate,
   unleash, empower and revolutionize.
7. Do not end by summarizing what the text already said. The last line must add information, give a
   concrete action or be removed.
8. Vary sentence length. Let short sentences land.

## Product and launch copy

- Lead with a concrete benefit or observable result.
- Prefer facts and numbers over adjectives.
- Make bullets verifiable from the product or its evidence.
- Use a specific verb-led call to action. Avoid generic endings such as "Learn more".
- Keep kickers factual and short.

## Final check

Before returning reader-facing prose, scan it once for every rule above. Rewrite violations instead
of explaining or excusing them.
